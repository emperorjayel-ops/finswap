Here's a question worth arguing about in public: when your exchange app says you “have Bitcoin,” where is that Bitcoin, actually?

Verified — as a matter of how the protocol works Native Bitcoin lives on one chain — the Bitcoin blockchain — and nowhere else. Everything else is a representation: a wrapped token on somebody else's chain, or a number on a custodial platform's screen that stands for a claim against that platform. That isn't a slogan; it's how the technology is built.

FinSwap is Omni-Present Solutions' answer to that question, and you can find it at energysc.org. FinSwap is built on the Bitcoin blockchain through the Counterparty protocol (counterparty.io) — the same open-source protocol that brought tokens and contracts to Bitcoin in January 2014, years before “smart contract platform” became a marketing phrase. FinSwap's assets are Bitcoin-blockchain assets, verifiable on Bitcoin-chain explorers, and the app links directly to Bitcoin's open-source code (Bitcoin Core) and the open-source Counterparty protocol, so anyone can see what the operation sits on.

The currencies — stated exactly

Two of FinSwap's currencies are Jay's, verified on-chain against the asset-owner records:

  • PERFECTCERTS — Jay's stated purpose a gold-backed currency; Jay's stated term it exchanges evenly for Bitcoin. Verified the asset's current owner of record is the same wallet address.

You will see other Bitcoin-blockchain assets discussed in crypto generally. FinSwap's public materials name only the coins whose ownership is verified on the record above — that's a deliberate choice, and it's how every claim here will be handled: verified, or labeled as stated.

Jay's position Jay also states that FinSwap is where members will buy, sell, exchange, trade, and invest across his ventures — developments, homes, cars, and land, priced in the currencies and in physical gold — all private and decentralized, with physical gold linkable to a holder's card through white-label partners. The honest label

FinSwap's app is live to visit at energysc.org, and its Bitcoin-blockchain foundation is real and checkable. If more platforms labeled their demos that plainly, the public record this campaign is correcting wouldn't be in the shape it's in.

The conversation we want to start is simple: shouldn't every platform have to answer the question FinSwap answers — where is the Bitcoin, and on which blockchain?
